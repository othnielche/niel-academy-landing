## 1. Presentation of the Start-Up and the Project

Every year, hundreds of thousands of students across Cameroon sit national examinations that will determine whether they qualify for university, a nursing programme, or a technical career. The majority of them prepare alone — with a photocopied past paper, no teacher on call, and no way to know whether their answer would pass a real mark scheme.

Niel Academy is an EdTech start-up built to close that gap. The platform works as a personal study assistant: students access a free library of past GCE questions and use a curriculum-aligned tutoring system to get step-by-step explanations, work through problems at their own pace, and receive graded feedback based on the actual mark scheme their examiner uses. Since launching in January 2026, the platform has reached over 1,000 students, with 503 enrolled in active GCE examination preparation. The platform runs on Android — available on the Google Play Store — with a web application in active development.

The core insight behind Niel Academy is not that students need more content. Past papers have always existed. What students need is a system that responds to where they are in their understanding at any given moment, and gives them the right kind of help — not just an answer, but a reason to understand.

## 2. The Problem

In a typical Cameroonian secondary school classroom, one teacher manages between 60 and 80 students. After school hours, a student who does not understand a concept has limited options. Private tutoring exists, but it is expensive and concentrated in urban centres. General study tools are available, but they are not built around Cameroonian syllabi or GCE mark schemes — a student who uses a general AI assistant for revision has no way of knowing whether the answer it provides reflects what the GCE examiner actually expects.

This is not a fringe situation. It describes the standard experience for the majority of GCE candidates across the country. Students in Mutengene, Kumba, Bamenda, or Mamfe preparing for O Level or A Level examinations do not have access to the same quality of academic support as students in Yaoundé or Douala who can afford revision classes.

UNESCO's Global Report on Teachers estimates that sub-Saharan Africa needs 15 million additional teachers by 2030 to meet its education targets. That number is a structural reality, not a projection that can be closed before the next exam session. Niel Academy is built on the premise that the most practical response is not to wait for those teachers — it is to give every student access to a learning tool that behaves like one.

## 3. The Innovative Factor

The question that shaped Niel Academy's design is one that most study tools skip: *how much help should this particular student receive right now?*

Give a student the answer immediately, and you support revision. Walk them through it step by step, and you build understanding. Push them to attempt it themselves first, with hints arriving only where needed, and you build the habit of thinking independently under exam pressure. These are three genuinely different outcomes, and Niel Academy is designed to deliver all three.

### Three Modes of Learning Assistance

Every question on the platform can be opened in one of three modes:

**Answer Mode** delivers the model solution immediately. It is designed for students in the final days of revision who need to move through a broad range of content quickly.

**Guided Mode** breaks the problem into stages. The student works through each step and confirms understanding before the next one is revealed. Progress requires engagement, not passive reading.

**Understanding Mode** — the feature the team is most proud of — requires the student to attempt the problem independently first. The platform monitors progress and provides a hint only when the student is stuck. A full solution arrives only if the student cannot resolve the problem despite the hints. This mode treats the AI as a support structure for thinking, not a replacement for it.

This three-mode design directly addresses the concern raised by educators that students using AI tools learn to retrieve answers rather than to think. In Niel Academy, a student who wants the full solution must first attempt the problem. That constraint is deliberate.

### The Technology Layer

The tutoring modes reflect an educational philosophy. The technology layer underneath is what makes that philosophy defensible as a product.

The platform's **grading engine** assesses student answers against the official GCE mark scheme — not a generic rubric. When a student submits a practice answer, they receive a mark, a model answer, a breakdown of what they got right and wrong, a note on errors commonly made by other students on that question, and specific recommendations on what to study next. That sequence — attempt, assess, understand, improve — is the full loop of effective exam preparation, automated and available at any hour.

Every interaction on the platform generates learning signals. The platform uses these to track which concepts a student has understood, where they are making errors, and what kind of support has worked for them — so the explanations and recommendations it provides become more accurate over time.

Other capabilities in the current version:

- **Curriculum-aligned practice** — questions are mapped to the GCE syllabus by subject and unit. Students select a subject and topic; the platform generates targeted questions from that exact area.
- **Continuous knowledge checks** — during a learning session, the system monitors comprehension and inserts short questions (multiple choice, structured, or essay) to confirm understanding before the student moves forward.
- **Photo-to-solution** — students photograph a printed or handwritten problem; the platform reads and works through it with them.
- **Open-ended study assistant** — students can ask questions across any subject beyond the current session.

**In development:** AI-generated video lessons, where students watch explanations of specific topics and ask follow-up questions in real time. The French subsystem (Probatoire and Baccalauréat) is also in active development, which will extend Niel Academy's reach to both of Cameroon's education systems.

### Why Existing Tools Do Not Solve This Problem

Students already have access to general AI assistants — ChatGPT, Google Gemini, and others. These tools can answer questions. They cannot tell a student whether their answer would pass the 2026 GCE Chemistry mark scheme, which units they need to revise before exam day, or whether they understood the concept or just recognised the answer pattern. They are not built for this purpose, and they show it.

Niel Academy is not a conversational tool with a curriculum layered on top. The GCE syllabus, the mark scheme, and the specific workflow of exam preparation — question, guided attempt, automated marking, targeted recommendations, next question — are the foundation the product is built on. That specificity is what a general AI assistant cannot replicate.

## 4. Project Summary

Niel Academy is a mobile-first exam preparation platform for secondary school students in Cameroon. It combines a free past-question library with curriculum-aligned tutoring across three learner-controlled modes, mark-scheme-based automated grading, and real-time comprehension assessment.

Consider a student who encounters simultaneous equations at 10 PM. She photographs the problem from her textbook, receives a guided explanation broken into stages she works through herself, completes a short knowledge check to confirm she has understood the method, and is directed to three similar questions from her exact GCE unit. She goes to sleep knowing what she got wrong and what to do about it. That experience — specific, curriculum-accurate, available at any hour, free at the point of access — is what Niel Academy is designed to deliver.

Since launching in January 2026, the platform has reached over 1,000 students and supported 503 through active GCE preparation — entirely through organic growth, with no marketing spend and no external funding.

## 5. Target Audience and Beneficiaries

The primary users are secondary school students preparing for GCE O Level and A Level examinations across the English subsystem of Cameroon. Within this group, the platform is particularly aimed at students preparing without access to private tutoring — which describes the substantial majority of GCE candidates.

Beyond individual students, the platform serves teachers and schools looking to extend academic support beyond classroom hours, and parents who want visibility into their child's exam preparation without the cost of private tutoring.

Expansion to the French subsystem — covering the Probatoire and Baccalauréat — is in active development. Once Niel Academy's model is validated across both Cameroonian subsystems, the same framework applies to other curriculum-based examination systems in the region, including WAEC countries. The starting point is Cameroon; expansion will follow demonstrated performance here.

## 6. Business Model

Past-question content on Niel Academy is free for all students and will remain so. A subscription unlocks the AI tutoring system, automated grading, curriculum-aligned practice, and the complete feature set.

Current subscription tiers are 500 FCFA/month and 1,000 FCFA/month. An annual plan is in development. Pricing is set below the cost of a single private tutoring session — deliberately, because the business case depends on accessibility, not premium positioning.

Revenue streams in development:

- **School and institutional licensing** — bulk platform access for schools to offer as an official revision resource.
- **NGO and government partnerships** — subsidised or grant-funded access for students in under-resourced regions.
- **Sponsored access programmes** — corporate or foundation-funded plans that extend free access to low-income students.

The business has been entirely self-funded since launch. No external investment has been raised to date.
